**Key 2026 rule change:** SBIR now caps per-company submissions (anti-"SBIR mill"), which structurally favors first-time applicants - i.e., PDL's clients and portfolio.

## Capability 1: SBIR as a shared service

**Why:** Non-dilutive Phase I money ($200-305K) maps almost exactly onto the cost of a serious EVT-stage engineering effort. For studio products, it de-risks R&D without touching the cap table. For clients, "we help you fund the build" is a service no dev shop offers - and it directly serves the Open Product Development positioning (grant applications require exactly the documentation rigor PDL already produces: milestones, budgets, technical plans).

**Build it:**
1. Pick the 2 agencies that fit PDL's product categories (NSF for broad tech commercialization; NIH/DOD/DOE by domain) and learn their 2026 solicitation calendars.
2. Create the reusable application kit: company/facility boilerplate, budget templates keyed to PDL's honest cost model, technical-narrative skeleton mapped to EVT/DVT/PVT gates.
3. Run one pilot application (a studio product or willing client) this cycle. Grade the process, then decide: in-house capability vs. partnership with a grant consultancy at referral margin.

**Service economics (typical market rates to benchmark against):** grant writers charge $5-15K per SBIR application or success fees; PDL can bundle it into engagements or price it standalone.

## Capability 2: Portfolio funding-path design

Every studio product and client engagement gets a funding path assigned at kickoff, matched to category:

- **AI-adjacent / robotics / industrial:** VC path is open - build the $5-10M seed narrative (defensibility, unit economics, production path) into the engineering plan from day one.
- **Consumer hardware:** VC is conspicuously absent (June data) - default to SBIR-if-eligible + Reg CF + revenue; design the BOM for bootstrap economics.
- **Niche/sub-$50M markets:** bootstrap-by-design; the r/hwstartups playbook.

Artifact: a one-page "funding path memo" template - category, ladder rungs in order, amounts, dates, and the proof each rung requires.
