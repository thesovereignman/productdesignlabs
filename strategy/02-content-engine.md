## Strategy in one paragraph

The research validated exactly which content formats earn attention in this niche: failure-forward devlogs on X, deep retrospectives and feedback-requests on r/hwstartups, open-sourced design files as audience magnets (FRAMEOSCOPE, 1,025 likes), and practical "where to actually apply" lists (the month's top funding posts). PDL's engine does two jobs at once: **every piece demonstrates Open Product Development by practicing it** (the content IS the proof beat the positioning currently lacks), and every piece captures a founder at a documented trigger moment (vendor evaluation, production-quote shock, post-burn rescue, fundraise prep).

## The four pillars (mapped to research evidence)

### Pillar 1 - The Open Devlog (top of funnel, trust)
The build-in-public practice itself. X thread + repo cadence for whatever PDL is actively building (studio products and, with permission, client work).
- **Format rules from the research:** numbered series ("PDL Devlog #12") for follow habit; struggle-first framing ("3 weeks of debugging, 5 broken PCBs" was the archetype post); open files where strategic - the repo star is the hardware waitlist signup.
- Cadence: 2-3 posts/week on X; monthly deep retrospective cross-posted to r/hwstartups (where the substantive audience lives) and the PDL site.

### Pillar 2 - The Burn Files (problem-aware capture)
The vocabulary of getting burned is up for grabs - the research found the pain is real but under-verbalized on social. PDL becomes the place that names it.
- **Anchor asset: "How to Vet a Hardware Development Partner"** - the checklist built from documented failure modes: DFM evidence, milestone deliverables, IP assignment clause, respin budget, reference-check questions. Gated or open (recommend open + email capture for the companion SOW template).
- Supporting pieces: "The Render Trap" (why beautiful prototypes can't be built), "What your dev shop's contract is hiding" (IP assignment), "Anatomy of a $80K ghost" (rescue case studies), "Red flags in a hardware quote."

### Pillar 3 - Honest Math (solution-aware capture, the lead magnet)
- **Anchor asset: The Real Cost Calculator** - inputs: product category, complexity, target volume; outputs: realistic ranges for prototyping, respins (2.9 baseline), tooling, certification, and an EVT/DVT/PVT timeline floor. Every number sourced from the intel brief. This is the site's primary CTA and the single highest-leverage asset in the whole engine.
- Supporting pieces: "Why your hardware seed is $5-10M, not $3M," "The prototype-to-production leap: where the $20K-and-six-months goes," "Budget 3 respins: the math nobody puts in proposals," "Landed cost vs. sticker price: the made-in-USA decision, honestly."

### Pillar 4 - The Funding Ladder (fundraise-trigger capture + studio deal flow)
The highest-engagement funding content on X was practical fund/accelerator lists for the rejected. PDL's version, maintained:
- **Anchor asset: "The Hardware Funding Ladder"** - living guide: SBIR Phase I ($200-305K, non-dilutive) → HAX/IndieBio ($550K+) → hardware pre-seed funds ($100-500K checks) → the $5-10M hardware seed. Update quarterly.
- Supporting: "SBIR 2026: the new rules favor first-timers," "Bootstrapping a niche too small for VC (and why that's a strategy)."

## The engine loop

1. **Devlog** (Pillar 1) earns attention and proves the process →
2. Readers hit a trigger and find the **Burn Files / Honest Math** piece that names their situation →
3. **Calculator or checklist** converts them to the list →
4. **Funding Ladder + retrospectives** nurture until they're ready to build (services lead) or pitch (studio deal flow).

Owned asset: the email list. Weekly send: one devlog highlight + one honest number + one thing we got wrong. (Frequency > intensity.)
