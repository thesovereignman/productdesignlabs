# PDL Storytelling Framework

**Craft sources:** Zinsser, *On Writing Well* (6e) · Forsyth, *The Elements of Eloquence*  
**Brand voice:** Brand Bible v2.2 · Fireflies · Jul 23 Marketing Sync  
**Hard ban:** Never use “once upon a time,” fairy-tale openers, nursery framing, or “we’re gods.”  
**Spelling:** Mohyi Labs  

---

## 1. Zinsser — nonfiction discipline (how we write)

| Principle | Rule for PDL |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Simplicity** | Strip every sentence to its cleanest parts. Short words beat long ones. |
| **Kill clutter** | Cut pompous frills, jargon, and empty intensifiers (*very, really, innovative, streamline*). |
| **Unity** | One pronoun stance, one tense, one mood, one attitude per piece. Don’t drift from struggle-doc to brochure mid-Reel. |
| **The lead works** | Open with a hard detail or a live question that earns the next second. Coax curiosity; don’t announce a theme. |
| **Build paragraph to paragraph** | Each beat amplifies the last. Last line of a beat is the springboard. |
| **Human voice** | Write as one builder talking to one viewer. Not a corporation. Not a fairy tale. |
| **Translate tech** | Make the constraint felt without dumping the recipe. Clarity without condescension. |
| **Business writing = humanity** | Memos that people can feel. Captions that sound spoken. |
| **Endings** | Land on a clean last image or line. Soft CTA, not a speech. |

**Quick cut list:** utilize → use · facilitate → help · innovative → *delete or prove* · paradigm/parameter → weed · “it should be noted” → just say it.

## 2. Forsyth — eloquence tools (how we make it stick)

Use these as *tools*, not ornaments. One strong figure per beat is enough.

| Figure | What it does | PDL example |
|--------|----------------|-------------|
| **Antithesis** | Clash two truths | Bench vs deck · print vs mold · clock vs physics |
| **Anaphora** | Repeat openings | “We print. We weigh. We fail. We start again.” |
| **Epistrophe** | Repeat endings | “…on the bench. …on the bench. …on the bench.” |
| **Tricolon** | Three beats | “Chicago. Ohio. Proof.” |
| **Asyndeton** | Fast list, no *and* | “Tariffs. Missing parts. Failed prints. Hot drill.” |
| **Diacope** | Word · gap · word | “Build the build.” “Trust the trust.” |
| **Chiasmus** | Mirror ABBA | “Don’t sell the deck. Show the bench. Don’t hide the bench. Sell the work.” |
| **Litotes** | Understatement | “Not a small ask.” (beats chest-thumping) |
| **Rhetorical question** | Pull them in | “Would you bring a printed airframe into that room?” |
| **Alliteration** | Light music | “Hard limit. Heavy lift.” (don’t stack) |
| **Aposiopesis** | Cut off | “If the printers fail—” (cut to restart) |
| **Hyperbole** | **Avoid for PDL** | No “gods,” no unverified “never in history” without founder Y/N |

## 3. Story spine (nonfiction — not folklore)

For every product arc, write four lines. No fairy-tale grammar.

1. **Cold open (lead):** concrete sensory fact happening *now*  
2. **Stakes:** what breaks if they fail (room, deal, buyer, body)  
3. **Mechanism:** how they fight the constraint (without IP)  
4. **Land:** image + soft CTA (Follow the Build)

**Plot card template**

```
LEAD: [hard detail]
WANT: [one concrete result]
FORCE AGAINST: [constraint / villain in plain English]
TURN: [what they changed]
PROOF IN MOTION: [what the camera can show]
LAND: [one line + Follow the Build]
```

## 4. Hook → Story → Soft offer (social)

| Beat | Job | Zinsser / Forsyth |
|------|-----|-------------------|
| **Hook** | Sleeve-tug in 1–3s | Lead with detail; antithesis or question |
| **Story** | Amplify with solid specifics | Unity; asyndeton for pressure; tricolon for road |
| **Land** | Trust without hard sell | Quiet ending; litotes over hype |

**Audience split**  
- **Interest (Mohyi window):** cool + trust — Follow the Build  
- **Buyer ICP:** capitalized operators with a real hardware problem — soft coffee later, not mid-sprint
