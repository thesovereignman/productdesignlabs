SUPALABS

The four signatures of the black box

Signature 01

The render trap

A beautiful prototype that was never designed to be manufactured. You discover it can't be built after the money's spent — the #1 documented hardware burn.

Signature 02

Withheld files

Deliverables held hostage until final payment. If the shop stalls or ghosts, you're left with nothing you can rebuild from.

Signature 03

The lowball

The cheapest quote wins the deal, then costs 2–3× after the respins nobody budgeted for. One founder paid $80K to a "local" firm that was a front — and got a lawsuit.

Signature 04

IP licensed, not assigned

The most-skipped clause in hardware contracts. You paid for the work; you don't own it. Every source-code-and-design-file theft story traces back here.

Questions

The parts other studios don't put in writing.

Do I actually own the design files?

Yes — at every milestone, not just at the end. Schematics, BOM, firmware source, and test fixtures transfer to you at each gate, and IP is assigned to you by default in the SOW. If we ever parted ways mid-project, you'd walk away with everything needed to continue with anyone.

Why do you budget for respins? Isn't that admitting you'll fail?

The opposite. The industry average is 2.9 board respins per project, and 90% of first prototypes miss their goals. Any studio that doesn't budget respins is either inexperienced or hiding the number until it bills you. We print it on page one.

What's the difference between the studio and the services engagement?

Today PDL does both — we build our own products and we develop products for clients, using the same open process for each. Client work funds the studio, and as our own products gain footholds, our mix shifts toward products. We're open about that trajectory because you deserve to know who you're building with.

Can we build in public?

If it serves the product, yes — public devlogs are optional for client work and standard for our own. Openness is a spectrum, and we'll meet you where you're comfortable. But the contractual openness — files, IP, honest math — is non-negotiable.

Can you help us fund the build?

Often. Non-dilutive SBIR Phase I grants ($200–305K) map almost exactly onto a serious EVT-stage effort, and the 2026 rules now favor first-time applicants. We help portfolio and client projects design a funding path from day one.
