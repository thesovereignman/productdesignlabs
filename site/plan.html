01 · TL;DR

One sentence, then the whole thing.

PDL is an open product development studio: hardware built where the client can watch — design-for-manufacturing (DFM) from day one, every file yours at every milestone, and the real cost math before you sign.

Every hardware research run pointed at the same customer: a founder who underestimates cost and time, can't evaluate vendors, and is terrified of paying for something that can't be built. Our enemy isn't a competitor — it's black-box product development, the industry model where opacity is the business model. We win by being the glass box. Today we ratify the category name and pick how far we open the box.

08 · What we decide on this call

Two calls, then we ship.

Decision 1 · Category name

Open Product Development, or Glass-Box Product Development?

Same enemy, same strategy — this changes vocabulary, not the plan. Jason favors A. Switching later is a find-and-replace.

A · Open Product Development (Jason's pick)B · Glass-Box Product Development
Decision 2 · How far we open the box

Pick the openness tier — the positioning dies if practice doesn't match.

Tier 1: contractual (files + IP + respin budget in the SOW, private). Tier 2: published process (calculator, stage floors, deliverable manifest — recommended floor). Tier 3: build in public (devlogs of real projects).

Tier 1 · ContractualTier 2 · Published (recommended)Tier 3 · Build in public

Also open: the proof beat (a live devlog + 1–2 case studies with numbers from our own builds), and the product-revenue threshold at which we stop taking new client work.
