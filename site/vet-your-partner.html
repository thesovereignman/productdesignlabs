How to vet a hardware development partner.

Eight questions, built from documented failure modes. Ask them of any studio — including us. An open partner answers all eight without flinching. A black box gets uncomfortable around question three.

1. "Show me a design you took from prototype to volume manufacturing."
The #1 documented hardware burn is the render trap — a beautiful prototype that was never designed to be built. A studio that skips design-for-manufacturing leaves you with something you can't produce. Ask for proof they've crossed the prototype-to-production chasm, not just made pretty things.
Red flag: portfolio is all renders and one-offs, no manufactured volume.
2. "What files do I receive, and when?"
The right answer is "everything, at every milestone" — schematics, BOM, firmware source, test fixtures, transferred at each gate. The wrong answer is "at the end" or "on final payment." Withheld files are how ghosting turns into a total loss: if they stall, you have nothing to rebuild from.
Red flag: deliverables withheld until the project or payment completes.
3. "Is IP assigned to me, or licensed?"
IP assignment is the single most-skipped clause in hardware contracts. "Licensed" means you paid for work you don't own. Every source-code-and-design-file theft story traces back to a missing assignment clause. Get explicit written assignment of all work product.
Red flag: contract says "license," is silent on IP, or reserves rights to the shop.
4. "How many board respins does your quote budget for?"
90% of first prototypes miss their goals; the industry average is 2.9 respins per project at $28–70K of true cost each. A quote with zero respins budgeted isn't cheaper — it's the lowball that becomes 2–3× the price once reality hits. Honesty about respins is a feature, not a weakness.
Red flag: zero respins in the budget, or "we get it right the first time."
5. "Walk me through your EVT / DVT / PVT plan and minimum durations."
Real stage gates have floors: EVT 4–5 weeks, DVT 8 weeks, PVT 4 weeks minimum. Beware "XVT" — the fantasy of entering a build with EVT-maturity parts while promising DVT results. Even simple products take 9–14 months to manufacturing-ready. A partner quoting months for quarters of work is selling you a timeline lie.
Red flag: compressed or hand-waved stage gates; a suspiciously fast timeline.
6. "What's your total cost model — including tooling and certification?"
The prototype is cheap; the overrun lives everywhere else. Production PCB iterations run $50–200K, tooling $50–150K, certification $30–100K. First-time founders run out of budget at manufacturing prep because nobody itemized it. Ask for the whole model on page one, not just the engineering line.
Red flag: quote covers engineering only, tooling/cert/production are "TBD."
7. "Who single-sources parts, and how do you handle tolerances?"
Single-sourcing components and skipping factory tolerance checks are the two named triggers for expensive respins. A good partner designs for supply resilience and validates tolerances before the build — not after a batch fails.
Red flag: no second-source strategy; tolerance checks aren't part of the process.
8. "If we part ways mid-project, what do I walk away with?"
This question exposes everything at once. The open answer: "everything produced to date is already yours — files, IP, documentation — you could continue with anyone." The black-box answer gets vague. How a partner answers the break-up question tells you exactly how the relationship is structured.
Red flag: any answer that leaves you unable to continue without them.

The one-line test

If a studio can't put its respin math, its IP assignment, and its milestone-deliverable schedule in writing before you sign, you're looking at a black box. The whole point of open product development is that trust doesn't require faith — it's inspectable.
