Not impressive. Inspectable.

PDL sells certainty in a category defined by its horror stories. So the brand's job is not to look impressive — impressive is what the black box sells. Our aesthetic is an engineering document: mono type, hairline rules, real measurements, visible corrections.

The Open Box.

An enclosure whose right panel has swung open, contents plainly visible. The enemy and the answer in one figure: the same box the industry hides behind, opened.

Clear space = width of the contents square (25% of mark height). Nothing enters it.

Never

  • Re-colour to anything but ink, paper, or redline contents
  • Gradient, bevel, drop shadow, glow — the brand has no shadows at all
  • Round the corners or stretch either axis
  • Hand-set the wordmark beside the mark instead of using the SVG
  • Place it in a circle or a rounded app bubble of your own making
  • Animate the panel closing. The box opens; it does not close
02 — Colour

Colour is information, never decoration.

Mono-first, with exactly one accent: the DFM reviewer's pen. Redline appears where a black-box studio would have stayed quiet — a cost, a callout, a failure, a number that hurts. Target: under 3% of any surface.

Redline is for

  • Annotation leaders and callout numbers
  • The stat that indicts the old model — 90%, 2.9×, $70K
  • "What we quoted vs. what it cost" deltas
  • The underline on a damaging admission

Redline is never

  • A section background
  • A hover state
  • A decorative rule
  • A chart's default series colour
  • The colour of a CTA that says "Get in touch"
03 — Typography

If it is a measurement, it is mono.

Inter Tight for headlines, Inter for body, JetBrains Mono for anything that is a measurement. That last rule is the most identity-carrying habit PDL has: it separates claims from numbers on every page, which is the entire positioning.

Both families are SIL OFL. Self-host them; never hotlink a font CDN in a client deliverable. This page is rendering on system fallbacks and still reads correctly — that is the test any type decision must pass.

05 — Imagery

The render is the enemy's medium.

The industry's signature failure is the beautiful render that cannot be built. PDL therefore does not lead with renders. Real benches, real hands, real light — and captions, in mono, on every single image.

Shoot / source

  • Real benches and hands. Flux residue, a scuffed anodised part
  • PCBs with test points probed; calipers on a dimension; mould steel
  • Failed parts, kept and photographed as evidence
  • Screens as they actually look — CAD with the tree open, a BOM in a spreadsheet
  • Process over product. Someone doing the work beats the object on a seamless

Never

  • Studio-lit hero renders on gradient seamless backgrounds. Ever
  • Glowing circuit-board abstractions, hands holding light, blue tech gradients
  • Anything implying a factory or facility PDL does not have
  • People pointing at a whiteboard
  • Duotone filters or a warm/teal grade

06 — Voice

An engineer who respects you enough to tell you the bad news first.

1 · Numbers before adjectives

Not "significant rework" — "2.9 respins, $28–70K each." A claim without a number is one the market has already heard.

2 · Name the failure mode

Every promise is stated as the burn it prevents. Files at every milestone because withheld files strand founders.

3 · Say the expensive thing

"Your project will probably need three board revisions" is our best line precisely because no competitor will print it.

4 · Cite the source

Stats carry a visible source chip. Uncited numbers are what the black box does.

5 · Short sentences, plain words

Expand every acronym on first use. The customer is technical-enough, not an expert.

6 · No hype vocabulary

Banned: cutting-edge, seamless, world-class, turnkey, end-to-end, unlock, elevate, game-changing.

08 — Before anything ships

The ten-second test.

  1. 01Could this have come from any other product studio? If yes, it's wrong.
  2. 02Is there a number on it? If not, why not?
  3. 03Is the accent doing a job, or is it decoration?
  1. 04Does it show the work, or the outcome? Show the work.
  2. 05Would we be comfortable if the client saw this mid-stream? That is the whole brand.
